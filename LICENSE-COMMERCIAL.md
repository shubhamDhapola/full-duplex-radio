## You do not need to pay

The PolyForm licence already grants these, for free, with no registration and
no obligation to tell anyone:

| Use | Covered by |
|---|---|
| Academic and industrial **research**, including publication | Noncommercial Purposes |
| Teaching, coursework, student and thesis projects | Noncommercial Organizations |
| Personal study, hobby projects, experiments, private entertainment | Personal Uses |
| Evaluating the project to decide whether to buy a commercial licence | Personal Uses |
| Use by a charity, university, public research body, public safety or health organisation, environmental organisation, or government institution | Noncommercial Organizations |
| Reading, quoting and reviewing the code | Fair Use |

Two points that are commonly misread, both resolved in your favour by the
licence text:

1. **Funding does not matter.** Use by an educational institution or public
   research organisation is permitted "regardless of the source of funding or
   obligations resulting from the funding". Industry-funded academic research
   is still noncommercial use.
2. **You may modify and redistribute.** The licence grants rights to make
   changes and new works, and to distribute them, for any permitted purpose.
   You must pass on a copy of the licence terms (or their URL) and the
   `Required Notice:` line at the top of [LICENSE](LICENSE).

## You do need a commercial licence

Anything done "in furtherance of a business or commercial purpose", including:

- Shipping this code, or a derivative of it, inside a product or service you
  sell, licence, rent or monetise in any way
- Running it internally at a for-profit company as part of operations —
  site intercom, warehouse or production-floor comms, crew or security radio
- Offering it as part of a hosted or managed service
- Consulting or integration work delivered to a paying client
- Use in a commercial pilot, trial or proof of concept that is intended to lead
  to a deployment

If you are unsure, ask. The answer is usually quick and is sometimes "that is
already covered, carry on."

## What a commercial licence gets you

Terms are negotiated per engagement, and typically cover:

- A perpetual, non-exclusive right to use and distribute the software in your
  own products, in source or binary form
- Removal of the noncommercial restriction for your organisation and its
  subsidiaries
- A patent licence on the same footing as the noncommercial one
- Optionally: support, integration assistance, protocol extensions, and
  priority on roadmap items you depend on

Pricing depends on the size of the organisation and the scope of the
deployment. Startups and small teams are quoted accordingly.

## How to ask

Send the following to the contact address above and you will get a concrete
answer rather than a form reply:

1. Your organisation and roughly its size
2. What you want to build, and where this code sits inside it
3. Whether you need source distribution rights, or binary only
4. Rough deployment scale and timeline

## Dependencies

A commercial licence from this project covers **this project's own code**. The
third-party dependencies listed in [NOTICE](NOTICE) carry their own licences —
Opus (BSD 3-Clause), Oboe (Apache-2.0), Catch2 (BSL-1.0) and nlohmann/json
(MIT). All four are permissive and impose no fee, but you are responsible for
complying with their notice requirements in your own distribution.

## Relationship to earlier releases

Commits published before the licence change remain available under the
Apache-2.0 licence they were released under. This is inherent to the earlier
grant and is not revoked. All releases from the licence change onward are
PolyForm Noncommercial.
